01 / ABOUT

The woman
behind the voice.

Some voices are heard. Hers is remembered.

Born on 28 September 1929 in Indore, Lata Mangeshkar grew up in a musical family. Her father, Deenanath Mangeshkar, was a classical singer and theatre artist who introduced her to music at an early age.

She became one of Indian cinema’s most celebrated playback singers, bringing feeling and precision to songs across languages. Through changing decades, composers, and styles, her singing remained a familiar presence in countless lives.

Remembered affectionately as Lata Didi, she left a legacy that belongs as much to the people who listen as to the history of music.

02 / JOURNEY

A life, in melody.

From early lessons to an unforgettable place
in the story of Indian music.

1929

A musical beginning

Born into the Mangeshkar family, she began learning music from her father during childhood.

1942

The first steps

After her father’s death, she worked in films as a young teenager, helping support her family.

1949

A voice finds its moment

“Aayega Aanewala” from Mahal became a major breakthrough in her playback singing career.

1963

A nation listens

Her performance of “Ae Mere Watan Ke Logon” became an enduring expression of remembrance and patriotism.

2001

India’s highest honour

She received the Bharat Ratna for her extraordinary contribution to Indian music.

06 / A PERSONAL TRIBUTE

Dear Lata Didi,

Thank you for giving
our feelings a voice.

Your dedication reminds me that talent needs patience, practice, and purpose. I admire the care you brought to every note, and the way your songs continue to connect people who have never met.

You may no longer be with us, but your music keeps finding its way into our lives—in quiet moments, in celebrations, and in memories we hold close.

With admiration, always.
Lata Mangeshkar
